# Product

**ECHO: The Ethics of Survival** is a browser-based cinematic narrative game and a prototype for a university course on social contract theory. In three short chapters the player is one member of a small group of survivors in a fictional outbreak, makes three decisions per chapter, reaches one of three endings, and reads an analysis of the path through Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau (with Popper and Castells in chapter 3). Tagline: YOUR CHOICES LEAVE A TRACE.

Research question: to what extent can collective security justify restricting individual freedom and moral rights during a social crisis?

## Users
Players who want a playable film, not a tool: a classmate or a lecturer who plays for about ten minutes, then reads the analysis and talks about it. Desktop is the primary target; tablet and mobile get adapted, not shrunk, experiences.

## Principles
- The game never tells the player what is morally right. No good or bad colours, no score, no ranking, no best ending, no correct answer.
- The player is a member of the group, not its leader, and no character's gender, ethnicity, age or disability decides who is worth protecting.
- Uncertainty is part of the design. Nothing the player can inspect settles the matter, and every ending stays unresolved, so no hidden truth makes a choice right or wrong afterwards.
- The model is open to inspection: four indicators are shown as words (never a total), and the ending screen lists what each decision did to them.
- Philosophy is paraphrased, never quoted, and the application to the player's choices is labelled as this game's interpretation. No philosopher is said to endorse an option.
- Evidence and memories are things the player finds and keeps (Memory Archive).
- Each chapter opens with a short content notice. The player can leave at any time.
- No external services and no personal data. It works offline.

## Anti-references
Dashboard, chatbot window, product-card grid, generic AI landing page, neon sci-fi, cartoonish characters, any scoreboard or moral meter.
